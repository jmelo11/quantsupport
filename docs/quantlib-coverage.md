# QuantLib index and calendar coverage audit

Compared on 2026-10-17 with QuantLib master at
[`5f4811e7e03bbd1a3d6d7df31b751fecd5daef5f`](https://github.com/lballabio/QuantLib/commit/5f4811e7e03bbd1a3d6d7df31b751fecd5daef5f),
dated 2026-10-16 (`QL_VERSION = 1.45-dev`). Recommendations below are based on
the repository's existing indices and currencies. Presence in QuantLib alone
does not establish that a benchmark is still published or appropriate for new trades.

The baseline tables below record the original source comparison. The priority
implementation is now complete:

- Ten calendar families and fifteen market/regional variants are available in
  Rust and Python; Excel accepts their canonical string names.
- AONIA, CORRA, SARON, NZONIA, NOWA, SWESTR, and TIBOR use local calendars.
- Nine new rate-index families plus EURIBOR 1W provide 27 new identifiers.
  BBSW/BKBM tenors are 1/2/3/4/6m; NIBOR 1/3/6/12m; SHIBOR
  1d/1w/2w/1m/3m/6m/12m. Arbitrary tenors, including 5m, remain future work.
- CDI uses effective annual Business/252 fixings. Business/252 excludes its end
  date and handles reversed ranges. Brazil settlement includes the November 20
  national holiday from 2024.
- Python constants, market enums, package exports, type stubs, and API navigation
  include the additions. Snapshot JSON persistence and selection are tested for
  all new identifiers and calendar-bearing OIS quote conventions.
- Python and Excel expose native historical snapshot selection. Excel archives
  complete markets with `QS.SNAPSHOT.FROM.CONTEXT` / `QS.SNAPSHOTS.CREATE`,
  selects via `QS.SNAPSHOT.SELECTOR`, and builds contexts with
  `QS.CONTEXT.FROM.SNAPSHOT`. Selectors choose timestamps and quote sides;
  index curves are requested from the selected pricing context.
- Day-by-day parity fixtures are generated by compiling the original pinned
  QuantLib C++ rules. Tests cover the fifteen new calendar variants and the
  existing Brazil settlement calendar.

After these additions, 34 of the original 44 missing national calendars and
23 of the original 32 missing named rate-index families remain. The historical
benchmark and additional abstraction recommendations below still apply.

## Baseline findings (before implementation)

- QuantSupport has five national calendars (Brazil, Chile, Japan, United Kingdom,
  United States) plus TARGET. QuantLib has 49 national calendars plus TARGET:
  **44 national calendar families are absent**, before considering market variants.
- QuantSupport also has `NullCalendar`, `WeekendsOnly`, and `JointCalendar`.
  QuantLib additionally has `BespokeCalendar`.
- Six existing rate-index families use `WeekendsOnly`: AONIA, CORRA, SARON,
  NZONIA, NOWA, and SWESTR. TIBOR also uses `WeekendsOnly`, although `Japan`
  is already implemented. These calendars affect fixing dates and overnight
  accrual periods, not just holiday reporting.
- Excluding the generic `Libor` and `CustomIborIndex` infrastructure, QuantLib's
  `ibor` directory has 42 named family headers. Ten have direct counterparts
  here; **32 named families are absent**. This counts families, not tenor classes.
  NZOCR is counted separately from NZONIA because their equivalence has not
  been established. NOWA, ICP, and the local SOFR variants must not be removed
  merely because they lack matching named QuantLib headers.

## Remaining index gaps

| Group | Missing QuantLib families | Dependencies or reason to defer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Additional markets | SHIR, BIBOR, PRIBOR, ROBOR, WIBOR | Add ILS, THB, CZK, RON, PLN currencies respectively, and Israel/SHIR, Thailand, Czech Republic, Romania, Poland calendars. |
| South African term rates | JIBAR | South Africa calendar; check benchmark transition and intended trade dates. |
| New Zealand policy rate | NZOCR | Distinct QuantLib identifier; establish its relationship to the local NZONIA definition rather than silently aliasing them. |
| Historical benchmarks | AUDLibor, CADLibor, CHFLibor, DKKLibor, EURLibor, GBPLibor, JPYLibor, NZDLibor, SEKLibor, USDLibor, CDOR, EONIA | Useful for historical valuation and migration, with explicit lifecycle handling. |
| Further parity candidates | MOSPRIME, THBFIX, TRLibor, ZIBOR | Validate benchmark identity, publication status, currencies, and historical use before implementing. |

LIBOR has permanently ceased, CDOR ceased after 2024-06-28, and EONIA was
discontinued on 2022-01-03. These should not be presented as live alternatives:
[Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/news/2024/october/the-end-of-libor),
[Bank of Canada](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/2024/04/carr-reiterates-that-market-participants-with-cdor-based-loans-derivatives-or-securities-must-prepare-cdors-cessation-post-june-28-2024/),
[EMMI](https://www.emmi-benchmarks.eu/benchmarks/eonia/).

Beyond the `ibor` directory, QuantLib has index abstractions missing here:

- `BMAIndex` for the SIFMA municipal swap index.
- `SwapIndex` and `OvernightIndexedSwapIndex`, plus named EURIBOR swap indices
  and historical LIBOR swap indices. A CMS/swap-rate fixing requires conventions
  and an underlying swap calculation, not just another rate-index identifier.
- Inflation indices: AUCPI, EUHICP, EUHICPXT, FRHICP, UKHICP, UKRPI, USCPI,
  ZACPI, plus the supplied year-on-year variants. These need observation lags,
  frequency, historical CPI fixings, and inflation-specific curve/product support.
- Generic/custom rate definitions, including separate fixing, value-date, and
  maturity calendars, as provided by `CustomIborIndex`.

## Remaining considerations and validation

- The added indices follow the existing metadata interface: currency, day count,
  reset frequency, fixing lag, and fixing calendar. Per-index business-day rolls,
  end-of-month flags, and value/maturity calendars are not yet part of that
  interface. In particular, BBSW's `HalfMonthModifiedFollowing` convention is not
  inferred by generic instrument schedule builders.

- Audit `fixing_lag` semantics before copying QuantLib values. The local SOFR,
  ESTR, TONAR, and SWESTR definitions return 1, whereas their QuantLib
  overnight constructors have `fixingDays = 0`. Publication delay, fixing/value
  date offset, and coupon observation lookback are different concepts.
- Preserve the existing serialized identifiers; add canonical names and market
  variants to Rust parsing/serialization, Python constructors and constants,
  type stubs, Excel help/catalogs, and documentation together. Excel calendar
  functions already delegate parsing to the Rust calendar enum.
- Retain per-calendar holiday overrides. Consider `BespokeCalendar` when users
  need a named custom calendar and configurable weekend definition; basic
  holiday overrides already exist locally.
- Validate business-day results against the pinned QuantLib implementation over
  a declared supported date range, including exceptional holidays, observed
  holidays, working weekends, and differences between market variants. Some
  QuantLib calendars rely on finite dated tables; do not imply unlimited coverage.
- Verify index conventions and at least one schedule/accrual example for each
  family, in addition to enum/parser round trips.
